How it works

From the door to the room. Without sight, on your own.

GPS stops at the street and a cane only finds what's right ahead. Isaac covers the rest.

01

Surveyed before you arrive

An agent walks the building's scan once. It names every room, checks everything a cane would miss, and writes landmarks you can feel or hear.

02

Door to door

Isaac lines the scan up with the street and picks the entrance, checked in aerial photos of the facade, crosswalks included.

03

Turn by turn, by touch

The phone on your back counts steps and reads the compass. The belt taps the side to turn, and the voice says only a distance and a direction.

04

Someone always knows

Your caretaker follows on a live map or by text, and hears right away if you ask for help.

Every hazard, checked

A cane finds the floor. Isaac checks everything above it.

The point cloud flags anything at waist-to-head height with nothing under it, and every wall the scan barely saw. Code walks every lead, so none is skipped, and a Gemini agent judges each one from a view rendered right at it.

1

Lead

an overhang or a glass suspect, from the point cloud

2

A view

rendered from the nearest panorama, aimed at it

3

A verdict

the agent names the object: hazard, not, or can't tell

4

The cane rule

tables and railings never count: a cane finds their legs

5

Evidence

the view is kept, and the router steers around it

Glass is what a scan misses. The glass walls and door frames on the JRR scan, the kind of thing the hazard pass checks lead by lead. At JRR it judged all 55 leads: 26 glass walls and doors, 2 open stair undersides and 4 things on the wall at head height are hazards; 23 aren't.
Agents

Agents in the back. Never in the way.

Before the visit

A LangChain Deep Agent surveys the building with five specialists, checks its own work against the router, and keeps what it learned for the next building.

place-indexerhazard-scoutlandmark-writersite-surveyorbuilding-cartographer

When something changes

A locked door or a blocked hall goes to a second agent. It proposes a way around in about four seconds and waits for a yes. Stop or turn back, and Isaac asks whether something is in the way.

The route itself is plain A*, in milliseconds. No model guesses during the walk.

Talk to Isaac

Call it. Text it. No screen needed.

A phone call

Call Isaac's number and ask for a room. The trip goes to the phone on your back, and the walk starts where you stand.
