# Site survey

## Reading the orthophoto
Vexcel orthos are straight-down aerial photos at about 7.5 cm per pixel, north up. You see roofs, not facades, so entrances are inferred from what meets the building at ground level.

**Entrances.** Look where a paved path, a sidewalk spur or a plaza meets the building outline. Typical signs:
- a canopy or awning (a different roof texture sticking out of the facade, with a shadow);
- a recess in the building outline;
- a widened, often lighter paved area at the facade;
- bollards or planters lined up in front of a door;
- a vestibule bump-out.

The main entrance usually faces the street with the address. Service doors face alleys, loading docks or parking with no paving pattern. Mark each with confidence: **seen** (clear from above or confirmed from a pano), **likely**, or **unknown**.

**Steps versus ramps.** Steps show as a series of parallel lines with alternating light and shadow across a path. A ramp is a long, smooth strip, often with handrail shadows along one or both sides, running alongside or instead of steps. If the facade path has stair lines and no ramp strip, the step-free entrance is probably elsewhere: check the sides and the parking side.

**Curb ramps.** Look at the corners and crossings: a curb ramp interrupts the curb line, often with a different-coloured or textured patch where the ramp meets the street. Truncated-dome detectable warnings show as a small yellow or red rectangle at the base. These are required at curb ramps by the Public Right-of-Way guidelines (PROWAG), not by the 2010 ADA Standards for buildings, so don't treat their absence as an ADA building violation.

**Drop-off.** A pull-in lane, a widened curb or a circular drive near the entrance. Note whether there is a curb ramp between the drop-off and the entrance path.

**Accessible parking.** Blue paint, the wheelchair symbol, and hatched access aisles (diagonal stripes) beside a stall. Under the 2010 ADA Standards, car stalls are at least 96 in wide with a 60 in access aisle, and van stalls are 132 in with a 60 in aisle or 96 in with a 96 in aisle (502.2-502.3). Accessible stalls should be on the shortest accessible route to an accessible entrance (208.3.1). Count what you see and give the side of the building; garage stalls aren't visible from above.

**Approach path.** Trace the walk from the nearest street sidewalk to the recommended entrance. Estimate the length in metres using the scale bar. Note crossings of parking lots or driveways, gaps in the sidewalk, and obstacles (planters, bollards, poles in the walking line).

## Interior cross-check
If a pano is in a room labelled lobby, entrance, reception or vestibule, one or two looks toward the exterior can confirm the door type (automatic sliding, revolving, push-bar, manual) and whether there are interior steps. A revolving door alone isn't an accessible entrance; look for a swing door beside it.

## Entrances under the ADA
The 2010 ADA Standards require **at least 60 percent of public entrances** to be accessible in new construction (206.4.1). Many older buildings have one accessible entrance that isn't the main door. That is exactly what a visitor needs to know in advance.

## What CoStar fields mean for accessibility
Field names vary in the kit, so read its index/docs and quote the names you use. Useful facts:
- **Year built / renovated.** The ADA new-construction requirement covers buildings first occupied after January 26, 1993. Older buildings without a major renovation are more likely to have non-compliant features (protruding objects, single steps at entrances, no ramp). Raise the hazard scout's strictness.
- **Number of floors / stories, building size.** Multi-storey means floor connections matter; ask whether elevators are listed.
- **Building class and type** (office, medical, retail, multifamily). Medical and government buildings tend to have more visitors with disabilities and more accessibility features.
- **Parking** (spaces, ratio, garage versus surface). Use with the aerial count.
- **Amenities** (elevator, fitness, food). An elevator listing confirms a vertical connector even when the scan missed it.
- **Tenants / suites / floors.** These become the directory: a name a visitor might say, mapped to a suite and floor. Only from the record; never invent tenant names.

## Writing the report
Facts with sources, in metres, in the sections the site-surveyor prompt lists. Every claim gets its evidence (aerial, pano id, CoStar field) and a confidence word. Put what you couldn't check under "Not verified".
